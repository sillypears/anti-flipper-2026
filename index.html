What ANTI FLIPR is, exactly

  • The rule. For every race and every poll in Silver Bulletin’s published data, the Democrat’s projected share is reassigned to the Republican and vice versa. Independent share is untouched. If FLIPR projects Hobbs 49.9 — Biggs 40.8, ANTI FLIPR projects Biggs 49.9 — Hobbs 40.8, and calls Arizona for the GOP.
  • Why. The working hypothesis of this page is that FLIPR’s read is, on average, the opposite of what happens — so the most honest use of its numbers is to read them upside down. This is not a statistical model; it is a mirror.
  • The limits. FLIPR’s published tables only cover races with polling averages (39 races today: 18 governors, 14 Senate, 6 House, plus the generic ballot). Chamber control cannot be computed from this subset — the seat tallies above cover only tracked races. The full 40,000-simulation forecast is not in the public data, so the mirror can only flip what Silver Bulletin actually publishes.
  • Refresh. On load, this page fetches the live CSVs straight from the public Google Sheet. If that fails, it falls back to the embedded snapshot (the build from this repo).

ANTI FLIPR 2026 is a parody/analysis project and is not affiliated with Nate Silver or Silver Bulletin.
